Cloudflare Turnstile (Bot Protection)

On this website, we use Cloudflare Turnstile to protect against automated access (bots) and spam. Provider is Cloudflare Germany GmbH, Rosental 7, c/o Mindspace, 80331 Munich, Germany.

What is Cloudflare Turnstile?

Cloudflare Turnstile is a privacy-friendly alternative to traditional CAPTCHAs. Turnstile verifies in the background whether a website visitor is a human or a bot, without requiring users to solve visual challenges.

What data is processed?

When using Turnstile, the following data is transmitted to Cloudflare:

  • IP address (anonymized)
  • Browser information (User Agent, browser version)
  • Device information (screen resolution, installed plugins)
  • Interaction data (mouse movements, keyboard inputs, touch events)
  • Verification timestamp
  • Referrer URL

For what purpose is the data processed?

The use of Cloudflare Turnstile serves the following purposes:

  • Distinguishing between human users and automated bots
  • Protection against spam and abusive use (e.g., in registrations, comments, forms)
  • Prevention of automated attacks on the website
  • Ensuring the integrity and availability of our services

Legal Basis

Processing is based on Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f GDPR (legitimate interest). Our legitimate interest lies in protecting our website from abusive automated use and ensuring IT security.

Data Transfer to Third Countries

Cloudflare is a US company. Data transfer is based on EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) in accordance with Art. 46 GDPR. Cloudflare is also certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Storage Duration

The data collected during Turnstile verification is stored by Cloudflare for a maximum of 30 days and then automatically deleted.

Right to Object

You have the right to object to the processing of your data by Cloudflare Turnstile. You can do this by disabling JavaScript in your browser. Please note that this may limit or prevent the use of certain functions on our website (e.g., registration, comment function).
